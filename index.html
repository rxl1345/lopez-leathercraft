Our Story
First Generation

From Oaxaca

My grandfather raised his family in Oaxaca. He fed nine kids with leather. Saddlery, belts, bags, small goods he made for other businesses to sell. One workshop. Every piece that walked out the door put food on the table.

First Generation

My Grandfather's Workshop

My father worked in that shop not out of passion but to help his dad and to help the family eat and live. He learned the craft the way you learn anything you do every day. It just became part of him.

Second Generation

By Way of Texas

When my father came to Texas he picked the leather back up. But this time it was different. It wasn't about survival. It was passion, tradition, and a feeling of connection to home.

Second Generation

A Different Reason

Leathercrafting became something of a hobby. A space for creativity and reconnection with the past. Keeping the tradition alive hundreds of miles from where it started.

Third Generation

Third Generation

I grew up watching my dad work on things in the shop. The smell of the leather, the sound of a piece of pecan tree branch he fashioned into a maul hitting a stamp. I wanted in before I even understood what any of it was or why he loved it.

Third Generation

Keeping the Tradition Alive

Today my father and I love working on projects together. Sometimes it's a belt for my groomsmen at my wedding, or a wallet, or a wet molded bag for my wife. Whatever inspires us to keep the tradition alive.

The Work
Batch I · Four Made · Dallas, Texas

The First Batch

Four available

Four crossbody bags. I picked this beautiful chocolate out from one of my favorite tanneries, Wickett & Craig, wet-molded each one for at least 24 hours, then hand-cut, stitched, riveted, and finished every piece myself. No two are the same, because the leather is unique to each section of the hide.

The beautiful thing about 10 oz English Bridle is that it ages. The patina gets better the longer you carry it, and it's one of the toughest things you'll ever wear on your body. The first one of these I made for my wife. She wears it every day. That bag is No. 0001, the first piece ever made under the Lopez Leathercraft banner. These four are Nos. 0002 through 0005. When these four are gone, the batch is closed.

The Hide

Wickett & Craig has been vegetable-tanning leather since 1867, one of the last tanneries in America still doing it the slow way. Their English Bridle is hot-stuffed with waxes and tallows. It is dense enough to hold a wet-molded shape for decades, and it ages into a finish no factory can imitate. All four bags came from one single side of chocolate.
